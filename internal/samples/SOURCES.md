# Sample recipe sources

Seven recipes from public-domain cookbooks, one per book. Each original was copied
from the fetched text listed below; nothing was quoted from memory. The printed
page's line wrapping is not kept: wrapped lines are joined, while paragraphs and
line-per-item ingredient lists stay as printed. The raw downloads
are not kept in the repository; the links below fetch them again.

All books were published before 1929 and are public domain in the US.

## 01-chicken-curry: Chicken Curry with Turmeric, Cream and Lemon

- **Book:** Hannah Glasse, *The Art of Cookery, Made Plain and Easy*, 4th edition, London, 1751 (first published 1747)
- **Fetched text:** https://archive.org/download/artofcookerymade00glas_0/artofcookerymade00glas_0_djvu.txt (Internet Archive OCR, Getty Research Institute copy)
- **Book page:** https://archive.org/details/artofcookerymade00glas_0
- **Location:** p. 101, "To make a Currey the Indian Way."
- **Verification:** checked word by word against the page scan https://archive.org/download/artofcookerymade00glas_0/page/n132.jpg
- **OCR and typography fixes:** long s (ſ, read by OCR as "f" or "{") is printed as "s". Corrected OCR errors: "{mall"/"imail" to "small", "skinthem" to "skin them", "ia a-clean" to "in a clean", "Sale" to "Salt", "ir" to "it", "puc" to "put", "mult" to "must", "itup" to "it up". Glasse's capitalisation is kept.
- **Image:** `data/01-chicken-curry.jpg` (1000×533, 163 KB). It shows the same recipe in the **1758 edition**; its wording differs slightly from the 1751 text ("beaten Pepper", lower-case "quarter"). The image is from the book, not a photo of the dish.
  - File page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Hannah_Glasse_To_make_a_Currey_the_Indian_Way_1758_edition.jpg
  - Licence: Public domain, Commons licence category "PD-old-100-expired"
- **Note:** the 1748 3rd edition (archive.org `b30501581_0002`, p. 101) prints a different recipe, "To make a Currey the India Way" (coriander, peppercorns and rice, no turmeric). The 1747 first edition was not fetched. This turmeric-and-cream version was chosen because it is the more cookable of the two and the scan shows it.

## 04-brussels-sprouts: Brussels Sprouts on Buttered Toast

- **Book:** Eliza Acton, *Modern Cookery, in all its Branches*, London: Longman, Brown, Green, and Longmans, 1845 (first edition; later retitled *Modern Cookery for Private Families*)
- **Fetched text:** https://archive.org/download/b21531869/b21531869_djvu.txt (Internet Archive OCR, University of Leeds Library copy)
- **Book page:** https://archive.org/details/b21531869
- **Location:** Chapter XV (Vegetables), p. 331, "BRUSSELS SPROUTS."
- **OCR fixes:** "Eich" changed to "Rich". Words hyphenated across line breaks ("vege-table", "pep-per") were rejoined. "salt : a" became "salt: a".
- **Other edition:** Gutenberg #72482 is the 1882 "New Edition", which revises this text ("Free them from all discoloured leaves…"). That edition was not used.
- **Image:** `data/04-brussels-sprouts.jpg` (721×1200, 83 KB). This is the 1845 title page, not the dish.
  - File page: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Frontispiece_from_Modern_Cookery_for_Private_Families.jpg
  - Licence: Public domain, Commons licence category "PD-old-70-expired"

## 06-parker-house-rolls: Parker House Rolls

- **Book:** Fannie Merritt Farmer, *The Boston Cooking-School Cook Book*, Boston: Little, Brown, 1896
- **Fetched text:** https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/65061/pg65061.txt (1910 printing, p. 58)
- **Book page:** https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/65061
- **Cross-check:** the 1896 first edition at https://archive.org/download/bostoncookingsc00collgoog/bostoncookingsc00collgoog_djvu.txt (pp. 61–62) has identical wording; only the ingredient layout differs (two columns).
- **OCR fixes:** none.
- **Image:** `data/06-parker-house-rolls.jpg` (1200×693, 143 KB). This is Farmer's own halftone plate captioned "Parker House Rolls; Salad Rolls; Clover Leaf Biscuit; Sticks." It shows the dish alongside related rolls.

  - Licence: public domain in the US (published 1910). It is part of a Project Gutenberg public-domain ebook. There is no Commons file page.
